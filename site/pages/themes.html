How it works

Inks, not colours — resolved every frame

The charts above were published once. A colour in them is an ink — $accent, $categorical[2], $ink@0.4 — looked up in the active theme when each frame is drawn. So a theme change needs no new data and no new page: the studio hands each chart a layer of tokens, and the next frame wears it.

Set a few, derive the rest

The built-in theme derives its greys, gridlines, surfaces and the ink on your accent from paper, ink and accent with colour expressions, and they re-derive in every mode. Change ink and watch grid and muted follow.

Three modes in one theme

Light, dark and high contrast are layers of the same theme: base tokens first, then the mode's. A brand that only sets its accent keeps the built-in theme's tuned dark surfaces; a dark-first brand says scheme: "dark".

Checked as you go

Contrast of text on its background, palettes that stay distinct for colour-blind readers, ramps that only get darker — measured on what the engine resolves, the way datars lint measures them.
