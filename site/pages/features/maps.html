Maps, no tile server

From the globe to a street corner — no tile server, no API key, no bill

Maps in datars aren't a second library bolted onto a chart library. Projections, region atlases and vector tiles are primitives of the same engine, so a country is a keyed shape like a bar, the basemap is a node in the scene, and one camera flies from space to a street. The basemap is one static file on your own host — made for you, for exactly the places your story visits.

Automatic basemaps

Street level anywhere on Earth, with one line

data: {
  // No archive to make: the build cuts one to this document's cameras.
  basemap: data.tiles.auto(),
  cities: data.values({ name, lon, lat, pop }, { key: "name" }),
},
scene: view({
  coord: { type: "geo", projection: "web-mercator" },
  camera: { fit: { geo: "=bounds" }, padding: 12 },
  children: [
    basemap({ source: "basemap", part: "base" }),
    instances({ from: "cities", x: e("geo.x(d.lon, d.lat)"), y: e("geo.y(d.lon, d.lat)"),
      r: e("scale.r(d.pop)"), fill: "$accent@0.72", screenSize: true }),
    basemap({ source: "basemap", part: "labels" }),   // names stay on top of your data
  ],
}),
  • Views from the document itself. Every state's camera and 48 frames of each flight decide which tiles, at which zooms, go into the archive — plus extra zoom levels around maps readers can explore.
  • Open data, fetched once. Natural Earth for the world, OpenStreetMap from zoom 10: coastline-assembled land, water, rivers, parks, roads by class, buildings from zoom 13, place names in the document's language.
  • A cache and a lockfile. Downloads go into a local geodata cache, so rebuilds are offline and byte-identical; the archive's job file records what it was made from.
  • Polite by default. Public OpenStreetMap servers are used within a daily budget; teams point the build at their own instance or a static cell server with one environment variable.
  • datars dev never waits: the preview shows Natural Earth at once and swaps streets in as they arrive.

What's in the box

Everything a map story needs

Choropleths as a join

The built-in countries atlas (ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 ids) ships as a compact topology, shared by hash across every chart that uses it. map colours regions from your table; bring your own regions as GeoJSON or TopoJSON.

Symbols, dots, routes, tracks

symbols at each region's visual centre, dotDensity scattered evenly inside regions, route along great circles, track drawn up to a moment in time, points and lines at longitude and latitude.

Projections a story needs

Web Mercator, Equal Earth, Natural Earth, Albers and Albers USA, Lambert conformal conic, an orthographic globe, transverse Mercator and national grids — plus planar coordinates for floor plans and fantasy worlds.

Your theme on the map

Water, land, roads, parks, buildings, borders and labels are theme tokens (map.water, map.land, …), so the basemap follows your brand and the reader's dark mode. The basemap is a recipe you can eject.

Labels that don't fight

Place names and your own labels go through the same placement engine, by priority, without overlaps; names draw above your data layer.

Credits handled

OpenStreetMap's licence requires attribution. attribution draws it, linked; datars lint flags a map that would ship without it.

What it saves you

No keys, no quotas, no per-view pricing

A basemap is one file next to your chart, on your host or CDN, read in ranges as the camera needs them. There's nothing to sign up for and nothing metered, it keeps working when a vendor changes its terms — and because it's the same engine, the flight in your iOS app, on your site and in the exported video is the same flight, frame for frame.
