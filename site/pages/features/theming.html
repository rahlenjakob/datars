Themed to your brand

Your brand, the reader's mode — applied at runtime

In datars a colour in a chart is not a colour: it's an ink — $accent, $categorical[3], $ink@0.4 — resolved against the active theme every frame. So a brand, dark mode or high contrast restyles every chart, including ones published months ago, without republishing. Type, sizes, corners and strokes are tokens too, and the fonts travel inside the chart.

How it works

A theme is a small file of typed tokens

Set a few, derive the rest. The built-in theme derives gridlines, muted text, rules, surfaces and the ink on your accent from ink, paper and accent, so a brand that sets three colours gets a coherent theme — and dark mode only has to change the surfaces.

import { theme, font } from "@datars/sdk";

export const acme = theme({
  name: "acme",
  extends: "datars/neutral",
  tokens: {
    brand: "#b3261e",
    accent: "$brand",
    paper: "#fbf8f1",
    grid: "mix($ink, $paper, 0.9)",
    "font.title": font.google("Source Serif 4", { weight: 700 }),
    "font.body": font.file("Brand Sans", "fonts/BrandSans-Regular.ttf"),
    "radius.bar": 2,
  },
  modes: { dark: { paper: "#15130f", ink: "#f2efe6" } },
  locked: ["brand", "accent", "font.title"],
});

Colour expressions

mix(a, b, t) in OKLab, alpha, lighten/darken in OKLCH, saturate, oklch(), on(bg) — black or white, whichever reads better — and references to other tokens and palette entries.

Palettes

Categorical, sequential and diverging palettes are tokens: a list, or generated from your brand colour. Data colours stay data — a party's colour is declared on its key, and the theme fills in the rest.

Modes and dark-first brands

Light, dark and high contrast live in one theme. A brand that is dark everywhere — a magazine with a black page — declares scheme: "dark" and keeps its night palette in light mode.

Locks

A layer can lock tokens so no later layer — a document, a host app — can change them. Overrides of locked tokens are ignored and reported.

Checked

Every theme gets a specimen board and a contrast check

datars theme brand.json --specimen out.png draws the standard charts, palettes, ramps and inks in all three modes side by side. Themes declare checks — WCAG contrast of text on its background, palettes that stay distinct under simulated deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia, monotone sequential ramps — evaluated by datars lint. Fonts travel with the chart

Your typeface, identical on every platform

Font tokens say where each face comes from — a file in your project, a URL, or a Google Fonts family — and the publish step downloads it once, subsets it to the characters the chart can show and ships it in the bundle, with its licence recorded. No runtime ever calls a font CDN, and no system font is used, so line breaks and label placement are the same in the browser, the iOS app, the Android app and the video.

  • Other scripts — Hebrew, Arabic, CJK — come from document fonts that join every label's fallback chain, with right-to-left ordering.
  • datars fonts doc.ts lists every token, its source, the face drawn and its licence; check reports a family with no source instead of silently falling back.

At runtime, in every host

The app decides the look of the charts it downloads

Hosts pass their own token layer and the reader's mode to charts they didn't build. Token overrides honour the theme's locks.

Web

const view = document.querySelector("datars-view");
view.setAttribute("mode", "dark");
view.setTokens({ accent: "#0f766e", "radius.bar": 6 });

iOS and macOS

let chart = DatarsChartView()
chart.load(.url(url))
chart.engine.setMode(.dark)
chart.engine.setTokens(["accent": "#0f766e"])

Android

val chart = DatarsView(context)
chart.load(url)
chart.setDarkMode(true)

The Android view follows light and dark today; token overrides are on the web and in DatarsKit.
