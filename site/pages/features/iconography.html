Iconography

Icons that wear your theme, on every screen

In datars an icon isn't a picture pasted on a chart: it's chart material. Draw any of the 2,121 icons of the built-in set (Lucide, ISC) or your own SVGs — one at a time, or thousands as one pictogram that a bar splits into. Every colour is an ink from your theme, never the file's own hex, so a brand or dark mode repaints the icons with everything else. Dials name their zones along the arc, labels follow curves, logos keep their colours as pictures. And a published chart carries only the icons it draws, with their licence: no reader's device ever fetches one.

Bars that split into their icons

One survey of 100,000 commuters, three ways. Each unit is keyed (mode, unit), the way a bar's parts are, so the engine sees every bar as the parent of its icons and morphs one into the other. A pictogram of a thousand icons is one node — one icon, drawn a thousand times.

Your icons, our inks

The theme paints the icons. The file never does.

A week's forecast in the built-in set's outlines — or in filled, two-colour SVGs of your own. Either way each colour is an ink: $highlight for the sun, $muted for cloud, $accent for rain. Pick a brand and the page lays its tokens over the published chart with setTokens(); both icon sets repaint, and nothing was republished.

Lay a brand over it

// datars, light: the theme's own inks
view.setAttribute("mode", "light");
view.setTokens({});

Three tokens and a sun colour make a brand here: the built-in theme derives its greys, surfaces and the cloud's $muted from paper, ink and accent. Your brand →

Bring an SVG

Your logo, your party symbol — in slots, not hex

Four party symbols from SVG files in the project, two or three colours each. datars reads each file's colours into ink slots in the order the file uses them, and the document says which ink fills each slot — here the party's own tokens. Restyle them all for a house style or for print with one call. The file's hexes never reach a pixel.

{{run:icons --slots site/figures/iconography/assets/fernmouth-first.svg|6}} 
icon("assets/fernmouth-first.svg", [x, y], {
  size: 44,
  inks: ["$party.first", "$party.first.mark", "$party.first.detail"],
  label: "Fernmouth First symbol",
})

Gauges and dials

Named zones along the arc, a needle that turns

The standard library's gauge writes each zone's name along its band — at the reader's text size, the band growing to hold it, or left out when it truly can't fit — and says the zone with the value ("62, Greed"). The needle is a wedge, a line or an arrow; it swings the long way round when the value crosses the dial.

view.setSignal("mood", 62);

Curved text

Labels that follow the ring

Any text can follow an arc or SVG path data. Each letter sits on the curve its baseline rides, turned with it, keeping its spacing whichever way the line bends — and on the ring's lower half it still reads left to right. A name longer than its arc isn't squeezed or run off the end: it's left out, and datars labels says so, so a recipe measures first and gives way.

text(d.name, [0, 0], {
  path: { arc: [cx, cy, r, a0, a1] },   // or { d: "M … C …" }
  when: e("measure(d.name, size) < arcLength"),
})

Pictures

The planets, photographed — and drawn to scale

NASA's photographs of the eight planets, each a picture in the chart: cut to a circle and sized by its true diameter, then placed by distance from the Sun, then at the end of a bar of its moons. Every photo is one keyed image(), so it flies from layout to layout with its crop. Saturn stays whole — a WebP with an alpha channel, its rings over the page in light and dark.

Jupiter is 29 times as wide as Mercury; Earth is a dot beside it.

{{alt:iconography-planets}}

Cropped, fitted, keyed

fit: "cover" fills each circle, crop: "circle" cuts it, and the crop is part of the node: it moves and grows with the picture through every morph. Saturn is fit: "cover" with no crop, in a box its rings' shape.

image("assets/planets/" + d.file, [x, y, w, h], {
  fit: "cover",
  crop: "circle",
  alt: d.name + ", photographed by " + d.by,
})

Decoded by your platform

Seven JPEGs and a WebP with alpha. The web runtime carries no image decoder: the browser decodes them, as ImageIO does on iOS and BitmapFactory on Android, and the tools read PNG, JPEG and WebP for exports and posters. The bundle's pictures are decoded before the chart's first frame, so none pops in.

The engine takes pixels, not files: a format your platform reads is a format the chart draws.

In the bundle, credited

Each file is shipped as it is — sized for the chart and compressed before it's added: datars doesn't resample or re-encode pictures. The document's credits name each photograph's licence, credit and source, and the build writes them into the bundle's manifest.

Accessible by default

Decorative unless it means something

Most icons repeat the word beside them, so they're left out of what a screen reader reads. One that stands for a word — a trend arrow that means "Rising" — gets a label, and is read as that word. Icons sized by the theme grow with the reader's text size, like labels.

Everywhere alike

The same icons on the web, in apps, in print

The engine reads each icon once, into paths and ink slots, and every renderer draws those: the browser's WebGPU, the iOS, Android and desktop hosts, and the exports. What travels

Only the icons it draws, with their licence

The build step reads every state of the chart, fetches the icons and pictures it draws — the default set from the toolchain, your files from disk, URLs once into a cache — and ships them as compact drawings in one chunk. The 2,121-icon set never ships, and no runtime asks for an icon: what isn't in the bundle draws nothing and says so. The forecast's bundle, each tier as its first load:
