{{count:recipes}} recipes, from bars to sankeys, maps to cards

The standard library covers what newsrooms, analysts and product dashboards publish — comparisons, parts of a whole, change over time, distributions, flows, networks, finance, maps — and what goes around them: axes, legends, titles, callouts, cards and controls. Every recipe draws in your theme, morphs by key, reads out to screen readers and plays on every platform. Try any of them below, on the real engine, with your own data.

bar() Bars from a category field on a band scale to a value on a linear scale (vertical or horizontal, decided by which axis is the band).

    The doc.ts behind it

    // Loading the recipe…

    Written from the document the chart is playing, and compiled back to it exactly (that's checked for every recipe). Save it as doc.ts and run datars dev doc.ts. What runs here: the engine's own recipe sandbox, in your browser — each change is a new document for view.setDocument(), expanded again and morphed to, the way datars dev does it. What needs the CLI: type-checking your TypeScript, datars lint, and publishing a bundle, which expands the recipes ahead of time so apps play it without the sandbox. Settings come from the recipe's own description (datars describe std/bar); the example is its reference figure's first step.

    One table, every chart that fits it

    Nine recipes, one dataset — and each morphs into the next

    Every mark carries the key of its row, so a column becomes its bar, its lollipop, its dot, its slice and its tile — and the parliament's seats and the waffle's squares are keyed by row and unit, so a dot splits into its seats, the seats gather into their slice, a tile breaks into squares. No transition code: the recipes and the keys are enough. Paste your own names and numbers: the published chart takes them through view.provideData(), on the light engine every bundle plays on.

    The example. Edit it, or paste two columns of your own.

    // In the document, the rows are a slot:
visits: data.slot("visits", { key: "channel", sample }),
// on this page, yours go in:
view.provideData("visits", { channel, visitors });

    The waffle and the parliament count percentage points of the total (rounded), so any numbers fit in a hundred squares. Figure source

    What every recipe does for you

    Defaults that hold up in print

    Text that fits

    Labels are measured with the chart's own fonts, by the engine, on every platform. Value labels sit above their bar, move inside when there's no room above, and are left out where they would be wider than the bar; legends flow into rows by their labels' widths. datars lint reports labels that collide or are cut off at the canvas edge.

    Scales from the data

    A plot builds its x, y and colour scales from the columns you name — band, linear, time, log — with nice ticks, number formats and axes sized to their labels. Marks inside it inherit the data and encodings.

    Themed, never hard-coded

    Recipes ask for tokens — $accent, $categorical[2], size.label, radius.bar — not colours. Your brand, dark mode and high contrast apply to all of them, including charts already published. Theming →

    Motion built in

    Bars grow from their baseline, lines draw on, slices and tiles morph by key, units split out of bars and merge back. Override any of it per state with motion rules. Animation →

    Accessible by default

    Every data mark carries a role, a label (“Sweden Democrats: 20.5”) and a value, so screen readers and text alternatives describe the data, not a picture. Accessibility →

    Composable and ejectable

    facet repeats any chart as small multiples; card places narration where it covers the least data. When a recipe doesn't do what you need, datars eject std/bar gives you its source. Extending →
