A bridge, a funnel, a stack

Three recipes in one document. Step through them: each bar keeps its key, so it finds its place in the next chart instead of blinking out.

plot({ data: "bridge", x: "item", y: "delta", title: "Revenue bridge, 2023 → 2024",
  children: [waterfall({ total: "total" })] }, { when: e('view == "bridge"') }),
funnel({ data: "stages", stage: "stage", value: "n" }, { when: e('view == "funnel"') }),
plot({ data: "revenue", x: "quarter", y: "value", color: "region", legend: true,
  children: [stacked({ series: "region" })] }, { when: e('view == "stacked"') }),

Live · candlestick · volume · moving averages · Bollinger · indexed · drawdown

Markets, on trading days

Candles and OHLC bars in your theme's up and down colours, volume in a pane of its own, moving averages and Bollinger bands computed from the full history, performance rebased to 100, drawdowns, and sparklines for a watchlist. The date axis runs on trading days — no gaps for weekends and holidays — and ticks on the first session of each week, month or year. Zooming from six months to one widens every candle instead of redrawing it.

plot({ data: "shown", x: "date", y: "close", xType: "band", title: "Nordhavn (NORD), daily",
  children: [
    bollinger({ source: "nord" }),
    candlestick(),
    movingAverage({ window: 20, source: "nord" }),
    movingAverage({ window: 50, source: "nord", stroke: "$highlight" }),
    volume(),
  ] }),

What every recipe does for you

Defaults that hold up in print

Text that fits

Labels are measured with the chart's own fonts, by the engine, on every platform. Value labels sit above their bar, move inside when there's no room above, and are left out where they would be wider than the bar; legends flow into rows by their labels' widths. datars lint reports labels that collide or are cut off at the canvas edge.

Scales from the data

A plot builds its x, y and colour scales from the columns you name — band, linear, time, log — with nice ticks, number formats and axes sized to their labels. Marks inside it inherit the data and encodings.

Themed, never hard-coded

Recipes ask for tokens — $accent, $categorical[2], size.label, radius.bar — not colours. Your brand, dark mode and high contrast apply to all of them, including charts already published. Theming →

Motion built in

Bars grow from their baseline, lines draw on, slices and tiles morph by key, units split out of bars and merge back. Override any of it per state with motion rules. Animation →

Accessible by default

Every data mark carries a role, a label (“Sweden Democrats: 20.5”) and a value, so screen readers and text alternatives describe the data, not a picture. Accessibility →

Composable and ejectable

facet repeats any chart as small multiples; card places narration where it covers the least data. When a recipe doesn't do what you need, datars eject std/bar gives you its source. Extending →

datars new mychart --template chart|story|explorable|map writes a working document to edit.
