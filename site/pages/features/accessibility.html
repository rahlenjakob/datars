Accessible

A chart a screen reader can read, not a picture it can't

Semantics are part of every mark in datars: a role, a label, a value, a reading order. The standard recipes fill them in — “Sweden Democrats: 20.5”, not “rect” — and each platform's runtime turns them into what its assistive technology expects. Accessibility isn't a layer added at the end; it's in the document, the bundle and every host.

On every platform

The native way, everywhere

  • Web

    A parallel, hidden DOM tree with ARIA roles and labels in reading order; a live region that announces each story step's narration; buttons for interactive marks; engine-drawn sliders offered as native <input type="range">; real links over the elements that link somewhere.

  • iOS and macOS

    VoiceOver elements with per-item frames, so explore-by-touch finds each bar where it is drawn; activatable marks; engine-drawn sliders as adjustable elements; macOS accessibility children.

  • Android

    TalkBack reads the chart through an accessibility node provider: virtual nodes with screen bounds, clickable marks, adjustable sliders. The node tree has been checked on the emulator; it hasn't yet been driven by TalkBack itself.

  • Desktop

    The same tree through AccessKit, for VoiceOver, Narrator/NVDA and Orca, with interactive marks as buttons and sliders adjustable — unit-tested, not yet exercised with a real screen reader.

  • Video

    WebVTT captions generated from the story's narration, alongside every MP4 datars video renders.

  • SVG and PDF

    SVG exports and posters carry titles, descriptions and roles; PDF keeps text as searchable text.

For every reader

Beyond screen readers

Keyboard

Charts are focusable; arrow keys step stories; interactive marks are reachable in reading order and activate with Enter.

Reduced motion

The platform's setting — prefers-reduced-motion, iOS Reduce Motion, Android's “Remove animations” — turns transitions into short crossfades and pauses autoplay.

Intents, not mouse events

Documents bind inspect and activate, not hover and click: inspect is hover with a mouse, a tap on touch, focus with a keyboard; activate is a click, a tap, Enter or a double-tap with VoiceOver or TalkBack.

A text alternative in every bundle

Every published chart carries accessible text — its title, description and each state's narration and data — and an SVG poster, available before any script runs. Every chart on this site shows it under a “Text description” toggle.

High contrast

Themes have a high-contrast mode alongside light and dark; brands keep their type and shapes while the reader's colours take over.

Colour you can tell apart

Themes are checked for WCAG contrast of text on its background and for palettes that stay distinct under simulated deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia.

Caught before publishing

The linter knows what makes a chart inaccessible

datars lint checks identity, encoding, legibility, accessibility and the theme: interactive marks without an accessible label, labels that collide or are cut off at the edge, more categorical colours than readers can tell apart, text colours that fail the theme's WCAG contrast checks, palettes that collapse under colour-vision deficiencies, rows without keys, and maps without the credit their data's licence requires. Agents get the same findings over MCP.
