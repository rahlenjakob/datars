Sweden votes, 2022

How Sweden's right won by three seats

The Sweden Democrats became the country's second-largest party, and four parties on the right took 176 of the Riksdag's 349 seats. The result, the long rise behind it, and where the votes came from.

By the datars graphics deskSept. 28, 2026

Sweden's 2022 election was decided by a margin so thin that it took days of counting to settle. When the last postal and overseas votes were in, the parties backing Ulf Kristersson of the Moderates had won 176 seats: one more than a majority, three more than the left.

The biggest story, though, was a party that would not sit in the government at all.

2018. Four years earlier, the Social Democrats had led with 28.3 percent. The Moderates were second; the Sweden Democrats third, at 17.5 percent.

2022. The Sweden Democrats gained three points and passed the Moderates for the first time. The Social Democrats grew too, to 30.3 percent, but their partners shrank.

Twenty-four years in the making

The Sweden Democrats' result was less a surprise than the next step in a long climb. In 1998 the party won four votes in a thousand. It crossed the 4 percent threshold into the Riksdag in 2010 and more than doubled its share in 2014.

Over the same years, the two parties that had long defined Swedish politics lost ground: the Social Democrats, who once counted on 40 percent, and, after their 2010 peak, the Moderates.

A party of the south

The Sweden Democrats' support is far from even. Their share was highest in the south and in the old industrial counties of the middle of the country, and lowest in and around Stockholm, on Gotland and in Västerbotten, where it was about half as high as in Blekinge.

Turnout fell three points from 2018, to 84.2 percent, still high by European standards.

The government that emerged was historic for another reason. For the first time, the Sweden Democrats formally shaped a government's program, 12 years after the party first won seats in the Riksdag.

About the data

Vote shares, seats and turnout are the Swedish Election Authority's (Valmyndigheten) official final results. The Sweden Democrats' share by county is computed from the authority's vote counts for the 29 Riksdag constituencies, as tabulated on Wikipedia's page of 2022 results, summed into the 21 counties (Stockholm, Skåne and Västra Götaland are split into several constituencies) and rounded to a tenth of a point; the national total comes out at the official 20.5 percent.
