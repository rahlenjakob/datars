Election 2024

How Trump swept the swing states

A 312-to-226 Electoral College win, built on seven states that were each decided by a few points. State by state, and county by county.

By the datars graphics deskSept. 28, 2026

Donald Trump won the 2024 presidential election with 312 electoral votes to Kamala Harris's 226. He carried all seven of the states both campaigns had fought over, most of them by a point or two.

It was not a landslide in votes. Across the country, Mr. Trump won 49.8 percent to Ms. Harris's 48.3 percent. But the votes fell where the Electoral College rewards them.

Every state, shaded by its margin: the deeper the red, the bigger Mr. Trump's win; the deeper the blue, the bigger Ms. Harris's.

3 for 3

The "blue wall." Pennsylvania, Michigan and Wisconsin, which Joe Biden won back in 2020, all went to Mr. Trump, each by less than two points.

4 for 4

The Sun Belt. Arizona, Nevada, Georgia and North Carolina went to Mr. Trump too. Arizona, by 5.5 points, was the widest of the seven.

19 states

Where Harris won: the West Coast, the Northeast, and a band through Illinois, Minnesota, Colorado and New Mexico: 19 states and Washington, D.C.

Margins are Trump's share of all votes minus Harris's, in percentage points. Source: county results compiled from official returns.

County by county

Beneath the states are more than 3,100 counties, and they tell a sharper story: a Republican countryside and Democratic cities, and a shift toward Mr. Trump almost everywhere.

More than 3,100 counties, each shaded by its margin, with the state lines and the largest cities on top.

Pennsylvania. Philadelphia and Pittsburgh vote heavily for the Democrats. Nearly everything between them is red.

Georgia. Fulton County, the heart of Atlanta, went to Ms. Harris by 45 points. Most of the rest of the state went the other way.

The Rio Grande Valley. Heavily Hispanic border counties such as Starr and Hidalgo, Democratic for generations, voted for Mr. Trump.

Where the votes are

Land does not vote; people do. A map shaded by county makes the country look overwhelmingly red. Drawn instead as circles, sized by the number of votes each county's winner won by, the cities come back into view.

Votes, not acres

The presidency is decided by electors, not counties. Sized by electoral votes, California's 54 dwarf the empty interior West, and the arithmetic of 270 becomes clearer.

Seven close races

In the end, the election came down to seven states, and every one of them was close. Wisconsin was decided by less than a point; only in Arizona did Mr. Trump win by more than four.

It was the first time a Republican had won the national popular vote in 20 years, since George W. Bush in 2004, and Mr. Trump did it with just under half of all votes cast.
