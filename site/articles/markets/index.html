Watching a market, minute by minute

The chart below moves as trades happen: one price a minute, the newest one still settling. Bring a free data key and it follows Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, the S&P 500 and bitcoin live. Without one, it shows a whole day of bitcoin and ether trading, recorded.

By the datars graphics deskOct. 17, 2026

What you're watching

Each point is the last price paid in one minute. The newest point is still open: it moves with every trade until its minute is over, and then the next one grows out of the end of the line.

The horizontal axis is the whole session, from the opening bell to the close, so the line fills the chart from left to right as the day goes on instead of scrolling past. The price axis moves only in round steps, when a trade breaks through one. The dashed line is the reference for the day's change: yesterday's closing price for a stock, the price at midnight UTC for a cryptocurrency. Above it, the line and the change are green; below it, red.

Stocks trade on weekdays from 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. in New York, except on market holidays. Outside those hours the chart shows the last session and says the market is closed. Bitcoin and ether never close.

Why it needs your key

Stock prices belong to the exchanges that publish them, and the companies that resell them for free do so on one condition: the data is for your own use. None of the free services we looked at allows a public website to show its prices to every visitor, or to store a copy for them.

So this page doesn't. If you paste a key, your browser asks the provider for prices itself and draws them for you alone. The key never leaves your browser except to go to that provider, and "Forget my keys" removes it.

Finnhub sends every trade as it happens, over a connection that stays open, but its free plan has no history for the day: the line starts when you open the page. Twelve Data sends the whole session so far, minute by minute, and then a new minute every minute. With both keys, the history comes from one and the trades from the other.

The recorded day

Without a key, the chart shows a full day of bitcoin and ether trading on Binance, the largest crypto exchange, from the daily files it publishes for anyone to use under a Creative Commons licence. It isn't live, and the chart says so. Press "Replay the day" to watch it arrive the way live prices do, 30 minutes a second.

About the data

The recorded day is Binance Vision's one-minute price history for BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT (data.binance.vision), licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The price drawn for each minute is its close; the day's reference is the first minute's open. It is in U.S. dollar–pegged tether, which trades within a fraction of a percent of the dollar.

Live prices come from Finnhub (finnhub.io: trades over a WebSocket, and a quote with the previous close) or Twelve Data (twelvedata.com: one-minute bars and a quote), with the reader's own key and under that reader's own terms of use. The status line under the chart says whether prices are arriving in real time, measured from each trade's own timestamp, or how far behind they are. Stocks are drawn for the regular session only. Times are New York time for stocks and UTC for cryptocurrencies.
