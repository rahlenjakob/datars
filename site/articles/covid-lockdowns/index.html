The pandemic

The spring the world stayed home

In seven weeks of 2020, country after country ordered its people indoors. How the orders spread, how many people they covered, and who never issued one.

By the datars graphics deskSept. 28, 2026

On Jan. 23, 2020, the Chinese authorities sealed off Wuhan, and then the rest of Hubei province: some 57 million people. Outside China it looked like an extraordinary, faraway measure.

Six weeks later it was the model for much of the world.

Seven weeks

Early March. Only China has locked down a region. The virus is already spreading in northern Italy, Iran and South Korea.

60 million

March 9. Italy extends its northern "red zone" to the whole country: 60 million people told to stay home, the first national order.

Two weeks later, Spain, France, Belgium, Germany, Britain and most of their neighbors have followed. Sweden relies on recommendations instead.

1.3 billion

March 24. India gives 1.3 billion people four hours' notice of a 21-day lockdown, from midnight. South Africa, New Zealand, Colombia and Argentina lock down the same week. The United States never has a national order; its states act one by one.

End of April. The countries that went first have been home for six weeks or more. Many are beginning to plan their reopening.

Shading is the number of days since a country's first stay-at-home order began, up to 50; China's counts from the lockdown of Hubei. Countries in gray are not in the dataset. Dates as reported at the time.

A third of humanity

Add up the populations behind those orders and the speed becomes clear. On March 8 almost nobody outside Hubei was under an order to stay home. Three weeks later, a third of the world's people were, most of them in India.

Europe's fortnight

The European wave took less than three weeks from first to last. Governments watched Italy's hospitals fill, and moved: Spain five days after Italy, then Austria, Czechia and France within the week.

What the orders achieved, and what they cost, is still argued over. Countries that locked down early and hard, late and softly, or never, ended the pandemic with very different records, and not always the ones their spring strategies predicted.

About the data

The dates are the start of each country's first national stay-at-home order as reported at the time. Definitions vary between countries (regional orders, curfews, recommendations), so treat them as approximate to a day or two; the Oxford COVID-19 Government Response Tracker is the authoritative record.
