The day oil was worth less than nothing

On April 20, 2020, the price of American crude fell to minus $36.98 a barrel. A pandemic, a price war and a town full of tanks got it there. The climb back took more than a year.

By the datars graphics deskSept. 28, 2026

For a few hours on a Monday afternoon in April 2020, anyone holding a contract for a barrel of American oil had to pay someone to take it. The price of West Texas Intermediate, the benchmark for crude produced in the United States, ended the day below zero, something that had never happened before.

It was the sharpest moment of a crash that had been building for weeks, as the coronavirus emptied roads and skies across the world and the oil kept coming out of the ground.

$46–$66

An ordinary year. Through 2019, a barrel of West Texas Intermediate cost between $46 and $66. Each candle here is one week of trading.

Below it is the oil in storage at Cushing, Okla., the pipeline hub where the crude is priced and delivered: between 36 and 54 million barrels all year.

−$36.98

The collapse. On March 9, after Saudi Arabia and Russia failed to agree on production cuts, the price fell a quarter in a day. By late March it was under $20.

On April 20 it fell through zero. The U.S. Energy Information Administration recorded the day's spot price at minus $36.98; the May futures contract settled at minus $37.63.

65.4 million

Nowhere to put it. A futures contract ends in real oil, delivered at Cushing. With refineries idle, the barrels had been piling up there: 38 million in early March, 65.4 million by May 1.

Traders with contracts expiring on April 21 and no tank to fill paid whatever it took to get out of them.

$55

The wildest year. The shaded band marks the ordinary range of the price: its average over the last 20 trading days, two standard deviations either side.

In a calm market the band is a few dollars wide. In May 2020 it stretched from minus $10 to $45.

$17.36

An American problem. Brent, the North Sea crude that sets the price for most of the world's oil, is loaded onto tankers, and a tanker can wait at sea. It closed at $17.36 on April 20 and never went below zero.

Set both to 100 on Jan. 2, and the two prices move as one until the day Cushing ran out of room.

−156%

Less than nothing. Measured from its 2019 high of $66.24, West Texas Intermediate lost more than its whole value: on April 20 it stood 156 percent below it.

The price did not get back to that high until May 17, 2021, more than a year later.

$73.52

The way back. The red line is the average of the last 10 weeks. The price was back above $40 by late June 2020 and above $60 by February 2021, as vaccines arrived and people started to travel again.

By the end of June 2021 a barrel cost $73.52, more than before the pandemic, and the tanks at Cushing were down to under 40 million barrels.

Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration, daily spot prices and weekly stocks at Cushing, Okla.

Not only crude

The shock ran through every fuel made from oil. Heating oil ended 2020 30 percent cheaper than it began; gasoline in New York Harbor, 18 percent. Only natural gas, which trades in a market of its own, ended the year higher.

The negative price lasted a single day. The next day the spot price was back above zero, at $8.91; by the end of the week it was $15.99. The day still changed how the market works: exchanges and brokers had to make sure their systems could handle a price below zero, and funds that held contracts close to expiry, like the United States Oil Fund, moved their money into later months.

About the data

Prices are daily spot prices from the U.S. Energy Information Administration, as published through the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis's FRED service (series DCOILWTICO, DCOILBRENTEU, DGASNYH, DHOILNYH and DHHNGSP). The weekly candles are built from the daily closes of each week, ending on Friday. Stocks at Cushing are the EIA's weekly series W_EPC0_SAX_YCUOK_MBBL, in millions of barrels. EIA data are in the public domain; the files and the steps from them to the charts are in the article's data/ folder and SOURCES.md.

The futures settlement of −$37.63 is the New York Mercantile Exchange's for the May 2020 contract; the charts show the EIA's spot price, −$36.98.
