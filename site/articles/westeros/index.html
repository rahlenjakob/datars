The Seven Kingdoms, charted

A map of a continent that does not exist: the great houses that rule it, the road that starts the story, and the television show that made it the most-watched drama of its time.

By the datars graphics deskSept. 28, 2026

George R. R. Martin's Westeros is a continent he has compared in size to South America. His readers have been drawing it for 30 years. This map is drawn by hand for this page: ten regions, a dozen castles and one road, each shaded in the colors of the house that holds it.

The realm

Westeros, shaded by the great house that rules each region, at the start of the story.

The North, the largest of the kingdoms, ruled from Winterfell by House Stark.

The Wall closes the North off from the wild lands beyond it, held by the Night's Watch from Castle Black.

King's Landing, the capital, in the Crownlands, with Dragonstone off the coast.

Dorne, the southernmost kingdom, ruled by House Martell from Sunspear.

The iron and the gold: the raiders of the Iron Islands, and the Lannisters of Casterly Rock.

A hand-drawn map for this page, approximate, not an official map.

Down the Kingsroad

The story starts with a journey. The king comes north to Winterfell, and his oldest friend, Ned Stark, goes south with him to serve as his Hand. The road they take runs the length of the realm.

The show

On screen, the story ran for eight seasons and 73 episodes, from 2011 to 2019. The last two seasons were shorter, with seven and six episodes, and bigger than ever: every finale drew more viewers than the one before it, up to 13.6 million Americans watching the last episode when it aired.

About the data

The map of Westeros is hand-drawn for this page as GeoJSON: approximate, not an official map. Episode counts and finale audiences (Nielsen, live and same day) are as widely reported. Game of Thrones is HBO's; A Song of Ice and Fire is George R. R. Martin's.
