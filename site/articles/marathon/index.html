The race against two hours

Fifty-six years of marathon world records, and the flat, fast city where half of the men's records fell. How the fastest times closed in on a barrier once thought unbreakable.

By the datars graphics deskSept. 28, 2026

In 1967, Derek Clayton of Australia ran the first marathon under 2 hours 10 minutes. In 2023, Kelvin Kiptum of Kenya ran 2:00:35 in Chicago, 35 seconds from a barrier that a generation ago belonged to science fiction. Four months later Kiptum, 24, was killed in a road accident in Kenya. His record still stands.

9 minutes

Every record since 1967. Each step down is a new world record, in minutes; 120 is two hours. The men's record has fallen by nine minutes, the women's by 11 since 1985.

2:00:35

The men. Eliud Kipchoge broke the record in Berlin in 2018 and again in 2022. Kiptum took 34 seconds off it in Chicago a year later.

2:09:56

The women. Paula Radcliffe's 2:15:25 in London in 2003 stood for 16 years. Since 2019 the record has fallen three times, most recently to Ruth Chepngetich's 2:09:56 in Chicago in 2024, the first women's marathon under 2:10.

Source: World Athletics, marathon world record progression (road records as ratified).

Faster, decade by decade

The record did not fall at a steady pace. It stood still through the 1970s, then came down in bursts, and the 2010s took more off it than any decade before, as pacing and shoe technology changed the event.

Where records fall

Records are made on fast courses as much as by fast legs: flat, cool and big enough to attract the best fields and the best pacemakers. One city has come to dominate. Nine of the 18 men's records since 1967 were set in Berlin; for women, Chicago leads.

About the data
